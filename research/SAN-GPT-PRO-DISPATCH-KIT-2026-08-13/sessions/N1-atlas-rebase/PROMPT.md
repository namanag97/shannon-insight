# Session N1 — globally researched non-data domain atlas rebase

## Commission

Produce a globally defensible release candidate for the universal non-data domain
atlas needed to assemble arbitrary enterprise applications alongside the separate
data-engineering and analytics family.

Return this artifact:

```text
SAN-UNIVERSAL-NON-DATA-DOMAIN-ATLAS-v0.2.0-RC.zip
```

This is domain discovery, evidence reconciliation, boundary adjudication, and corpus
engineering. It is not library generation and it is not compiler implementation.

## Governing intent

The attached `BASE-RESEARCH-CONSTITUTION.md` is binding. The result must support a
future chain from declared enterprise intent to accepted domain specifications,
admissible libraries, compiler contributions, target lowering, runtime/provider
binding, and observable proof. Do not collapse those artifact kinds.

The atlas must describe the world broadly enough that later work can identify the
reusable horizontal semantic owners, industry vertical profiles, application and
product compositions, technical mechanisms, effectful boundaries, runtime/resource
roles, provider roles, compiler/tooling roles, and data/analytics seams required by
real enterprise systems. A name appearing in an input is not proof that it is a
bounded context or a library.

## Research authority and scope

Use the attached archives as candidate evidence, not as the limit of the research.
Research the current Internet broadly and globally. Seek primary standards,
specifications, legislation only where software semantics depend on it, academic
fields, mature open-source ecosystems, industry reference models, and practitioner
evidence across regions and industries. Record evidence at claim level, including
contradictions, scope, jurisdiction, dates, and negative findings.

The scope is software needed to build enterprise applications and data-enabled
enterprise solutions. Do not attempt to solve law, regulation, organizational policy,
or industry strategy. Do model the semantics, decisions, obligations, evidence, and
variation software must represent or enforce. Keep data engineering and analytics as
a neighboring sovereign family; map its seams without duplicating its domain corpus.

## Required result

The v0.2.0 release candidate must:

- reingest every record from every attached source corpus and preserve source lineage;
- account explicitly for every v0.1.0 atlas record as retained, corrected, split,
  merged, reclassified, deferred, rejected, or superseded;
- recover material detail lost by prior normalization and report loss/repair evidence;
- provide a globally researched universe of candidate domain families, bounded
  contexts, foundations, profiles, compositions, technical dispositions, applications,
  runtime/resource roles, providers, compiler/tooling roles, and industry mappings;
- distinguish semantic authority from capability, workflow, implementation, package,
  provider, product, industry label, and research topic;
- give every proposed semantic owner one stable sovereign question, positive scope,
  negative scope, ubiquitous language, named consumers, upstream/downstream neighbors,
  typed context relationships, anti-corruption needs, published language candidates,
  laws, lifecycle evidence, refusals, and unresolved uncertainty;
- represent foundation/horizontal/vertical and semantic/mechanism/provider/application
  axes independently rather than forcing one hierarchy to answer every question;
- map every candidate against the attached global registry as an exact match, alias,
  merge, split, reclassification, new-unit proposal, conflict, deferment, or rejection;
- preserve disputes and false twins instead of resolving ambiguity through naming;
- test boundaries against the attached cross-domain competencies and add missing
  competency coverage revealed by global research;
- evaluate coverage across world regions, enterprise scales, deployment models,
  industries and subindustries, public/private/nonprofit organizations, physical and
  digital operations, regulated and unregulated contexts, human and automated work,
  and positive/negative compositions;
- make every completeness and maturity claim measurable and reproducible;
- emit work-package seeds for later Domain Forges without asserting that a candidate
  context is accepted or maps one-to-one to a library.

## Acceptance conditions

The archive is acceptable only if it is deterministic, internally closed, navigable,
and independently auditable. It must include closed schemas and vocabularies, a
manifest, checksums, generated indexes, source/claim/conflict/gap ledgers, coverage
matrices, boundary and ownership decisions, and a validator that reproduces all
reported mechanical claims.

Validation must cover more than JSON parsing. It must check schema closure,
identifiers, references, allowed states, duplicate and near-duplicate handling,
deterministic ordering, lineage, relation endpoint/kind constraints, illegal cycles,
evidence minimums, registry crosswalk closure, competency accounting, generated
counts, and checksums. Report separately what remains a human or expert judgment.

No candidate may be promoted merely because the input called it complete, canonical,
validated, or mature. No domain, context, or library count is a success criterion.
Prefer a smaller justified accepted set plus explicit unresolved candidates over a
large false ontology.
