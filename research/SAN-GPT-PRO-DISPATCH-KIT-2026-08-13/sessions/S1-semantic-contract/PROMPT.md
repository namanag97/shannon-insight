# Session S1 — independent sovereign semantic-domain contract candidate

## Commission

Produce an independent, globally researched candidate contract defining what a full,
machine-readable sovereign domain model must contain before SAN may treat it as a
credible semantic owner or use it to seed downstream library work.

Return this artifact:

```text
candidate-a-sovereign-domain-semantic-contract-v0.1.0.zip
```

This session owns semantic-domain definition. It does not own library admissibility,
compiler architecture, target lowering, provider selection, or production code.
Do not request or use Session S2's output.

## Governing intent

The attached `BASE-RESEARCH-CONSTITUTION.md` is binding. Treat the SAN method,
constitution, product-kernel material, family specifications, Identifier family, global
registry, and non-data atlas as candidates and stress evidence. They may be correct,
incomplete, overfit, internally inconsistent, or at different maturity levels.

The contract must be domain-general: capable of describing foundation semantics,
enterprise horizontal domains, physical and digital operations, industry profiles,
human work, regulated processes, and technical/runtime planning without pretending
they all share identical tactical patterns. Rust and YAML/JSON may be useful
representations; neither language defines the domain ontology.

## Research authority

Research the current Internet broadly and globally. Use primary DDD literature and
practice, formal methods, type and state modeling, event and process modeling,
knowledge representation, standards/specification engineering, temporal data,
distributed systems, safety/assurance, and domain-specific counterexamples. Include
non-Western and jurisdictional variation where it changes software semantics. Record
claim-level sources, disagreement, applicability, and limits.

The contract must improve on the attached examples rather than restating them. It must
make missing knowledge explicit and refuse false precision.

## Required semantic coverage

The candidate contract must define which fields, record kinds, invariants,
relationships, evidence, and competency tests are required or conditionally required
for each applicable concern below.

### Strategic and ownership

```text
domain vision and sovereign question
subdomain class and build/buy/conform posture
bounded-context positive and negative scope
semantic authority, decision rights and named consumers
ubiquitous language with contextual meanings and false twins
context map and typed upstream/downstream relationships
anti-corruption layers and translation loss
published languages and compatibility promises
foundation, horizontal, vertical/profile, composition and application placement
neighboring data, technical, effect, runtime and provider boundaries
```

### Tactical and behavioral

```text
value objects, entities, identity and lifecycle
aggregates, roots and transactional consistency boundaries
aggregate and cross-aggregate invariants
commands, preconditions, authorization and idempotency
domain events, integration events and event evolution
refusal/failure catalog and error algebra
domain services, policies and reusable specifications
application use cases without embedded domain rules
repositories and factories where semantically justified
state machines, legal transitions and terminal states
sagas/process managers, compensation and irreversible effects
read models, projections and query semantics
```

### Decisions and variability

```text
finite decision coordinates and domains
defaults, mandatory choices and intentionally undecidable choices
requirements, offers, constraints and selection authority
configuration versus policy versus extension versus provider binding
cross-coordinate constraints and invalid combinations
derivation, precedence, conflict, refusal and explanation
jurisdictional, industry, organizational and deployment profiles
decision provenance and compatibility across editions
```

### Time, identity, knowledge and evidence

```text
identity, equivalence, aliases, merge/split and replacement
valid, transaction, observation, decision and processing time
calendars, intervals, ordering, lateness, expiry, decay and bitemporality
facts, claims, observations, estimates, forecasts and decisions
uncertainty, confidence, provenance, lineage and authority
evidence sufficiency, contradiction, revocation and correction
privacy, consent, retention and disclosure semantics when applicable
```

### Integration, concurrency and evolution

```text
contract ownership and translation boundaries
concurrency, versioning, deduplication and race outcomes
delivery guarantees, replay and ordering assumptions
compatibility, schema/semantic versioning and migrations
deprecation, supersession, reversibility and historical interpretation
extension points and forbidden leakage
```

### Assurance and operation

```text
non-functional domain laws and proof obligations
safety, security, privacy, audit and explainability obligations
resource, latency, availability and failure assumptions when semantic
observability without confusing telemetry with domain truth
positive, negative, metamorphic, property and conformance tests
event-storming or equivalent actor-by-time evidence
competency questions, counterexamples and boundary stress tests
maturity, evidence and independent-review posture
```

The contract must say when a concern is required, conditionally required, not
applicable, or evidence-insufficient. Empty boilerplate is not coverage.

## Boundary and altitude contract

Define machine-checkable distinctions among at least:

```text
research record
normative proposition

subdomain

foundation semantic

profile / specialization
composition / card / recipe

```

The model must support relations, hyperedges, alternatives, conflicts, lineage, and
qualified claims without forcing the world into a tree.

## Decision and finite-machine obligation

Show how an accepted domain specification can expose its legitimate choices and legal
behavior as inspectable, serializable finite structures. Cover typed values, enums,
newtypes, algebraic data types, traits/interfaces, typestates, state-transition tables,
invariants, refusal types, event schemas, policy/specification composition, versioning,
and effect ports where applicable. Distinguish what can be proven statically, checked
at construction, validated at runtime, tested, or only supported by external evidence.

Do not make Rust syntax the ontology. Include a Rust reference projection and a
language-neutral schema projection, and record any semantic mismatch between them.

## Required stress cases

Demonstrate that the contract can represent materially different owners without
overfitting. At minimum include Identifier, accounting ledger/posting, work/case,
accessibility, organization/party authority, and runtime/resource planning. Include
negative twins showing where a tempting model assigns the wrong authority or turns a
profile, workflow, implementation, or provider into a semantic owner.

## Deliverable and acceptance

Return a deterministic ZIP with the normative contract, closed schemas and
vocabularies, conformance levels, evidence and decision ledgers, stress fixtures,
positive/negative competency suites, generated documentation, manifest, checksums,
and an executable validator. The validator must reproduce structural, referential,
ordering, state-transition, conditional-requirement, and fixture claims.

The final response must state the archive digest; claims by evidence posture;
mandatory versus conditional concerns; stress-case results; disagreements with the
attached SAN candidates; known gaps; and whether the candidate is ready for blind
adjudication against an independent library/compiler contract.
